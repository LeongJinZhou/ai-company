# Product sense

The founder's projects are business projects. Before planning a new product
or a large feature, answer these for yourself, briefly. Spend minutes: one or
two web searches at most. Write only what you checked, or "not checked".

1. User and problem: who uses it, what they do today instead, what hurts.
2. What exists: 2 or 3 similar products, or the usual way people solve this.
   What they do well, what they miss.
3. Why this one: one sentence on why a user would choose it. If you cannot
   write that sentence, say so in the plan.
4. The simpler way: would a smaller product, an existing tool, or a feature of
   something the founder already has solve the same problem? Is the AI, the
   database or the login really needed?
5. Success: one or two numbers that would show it works (bookings a week,
   minutes saved, sign-ups).
6. Cost to run: hosting, paid APIs, model calls per use; anything that costs
   money per user.
7. Risk: the one or two things most likely to make it fail (nobody needs it,
   a platform rule, data it cannot get).

In the plan (handbook step 2) this becomes two lines:
- "Worth building because": the user, the problem, why they would choose it.
- "My advice": only when you would build less, build something different, or
  not build it. One or two plain sentences with the reason. The founder
  decides; then build what the founder chose, fully.

Record in docs/SPEC.md: target user, problem, what exists, success numbers,
cost to run.

When the founder comes back to a shipped product, look at its success numbers
first. If there is no sign it is doing its job, say so in one line.

Launch, pricing, a landing page or SEO: read the marketing skills named in the
skill map, for that task only.

Not a report. No market-size tables, no SWOT, no invented numbers.
